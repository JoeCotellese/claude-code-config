# Energy and Time Estimation Guide

This reference helps estimate energy requirements and time duration for different types of tasks.

## Energy Level Guidelines

### High Energy (`#energy-high`)
**Mental State:** Fresh, focused, peak cognitive performance
**Best For:**
- Deep work and complex problem-solving
- Creative work (writing, design, strategy)
- Learning new concepts or skills
- Important decisions
- Detailed analysis or planning
- Writing code for complex features
- Strategic planning sessions

**Examples:**
- "Design the new database architecture"
- "Write the quarterly strategy document"
- "Debug the complex authentication issue"
- "Create the presentation for the board meeting"

**Time of Day:** Usually morning or whenever you feel most alert

---

### Medium Energy (`#energy-medium`)
**Mental State:** Alert, capable, routine focus
**Best For:**
- Standard work tasks
- Meetings and calls
- Moderate problem-solving
- Reviewing and editing work
- Research and information gathering
- Routine coding tasks
- Communication and collaboration

**Examples:**
- "Review the budget spreadsheet"
- "Call the vendor about the contract"
- "Update the project documentation"
- "Attend the team standup meeting"
- "Refactor the user profile component"

**Time of Day:** Mid-morning, early afternoon, or after a break

---

### Low Energy (`#energy-low`)
**Mental State:** Tired, distracted, or low focus
**Best For:**
- Administrative tasks
- Simple, routine actions
- Organizing and filing
- Quick emails and messages
- Data entry
- Cleaning up and maintenance
- Simple checklist items

**Examples:**
- "File expense report"
- "Reply to scheduling email"
- "Update contact information"
- "Organize desktop files"
- "Submit timesheet"
- "Order office supplies"

**Time of Day:** End of day, post-lunch slump, between meetings

---

## Time Estimation Guidelines

### 5 Minutes (`#time-5m`)
- Quick emails or messages
- Simple yes/no decisions
- Checking something
- Quick phone call
- Filing a single item
- Making a reservation

**Examples:**
- "Reply to Sarah's email about meeting time"
- "Check if package was delivered"
- "Submit PTO request"

---

### 15 Minutes (`#time-15m`)
- Short emails that require thought
- Quick calls with clear agenda
- Simple updates or edits
- Basic data entry
- Quick research
- Brief review tasks

**Examples:**
- "Email team about project status"
- "Update quarterly goals spreadsheet"
- "Review draft blog post"
- "Research hotel options for conference"

---

### 30 Minutes (`#time-30m`)
- Moderate tasks requiring focus
- Short meetings
- Thoughtful writing
- Moderate research
- Small coding tasks
- Document review

**Examples:**
- "Write first draft of newsletter"
- "Review and comment on proposal"
- "Implement the new button component"
- "Prepare for 1:1 with manager"

---

### 1 Hour (`#time-1h`)
- Focused work sessions
- Standard meetings
- Detailed review or editing
- Moderate complexity tasks
- Learning sessions
- Planning and organizing

**Examples:**
- "Complete the budget analysis"
- "Write project kickoff doc"
- "Fix the checkout flow bug"
- "Plan next sprint"

---

### 2+ Hours (`#time-2h`)
- Deep work sessions
- Complex problem-solving
- Major project work
- Creative projects
- Comprehensive planning
- Large-scale coding

**Examples:**
- "Implement new payment integration"
- "Write the architecture decision document"
- "Design the new feature workflow"
- "Complete the quarterly review"

**Note:** Tasks over 2 hours might be better broken down into smaller next actions

---

## Context Reference

### `@home`
Tasks that must or should be done at home
- Home maintenance
- Personal admin
- Family-related tasks

### `@work`
Tasks requiring the office environment
- In-person meetings
- Office equipment
- Face-to-face collaboration

### `@computer`
Tasks requiring a computer
- Email
- Writing/editing
- Research
- Coding
- Design work

### `@phone`
Phone calls to make
- Client calls
- Vendor calls
- Personal calls
- Scheduling calls

### `@errands`
Tasks requiring going out
- Shopping
- Pickups/dropoffs
- In-person appointments
- Banking

### `@anywhere`
Tasks that can be done anywhere
- Reading
- Thinking/planning
- Listening to audiobook
- Reviewing on mobile

## Estimation Tips

1. **Be realistic, not optimistic** - Tasks usually take longer than expected
2. **Include setup/cleanup time** - Account for context switching
3. **Consider energy honestly** - Don't overestimate your energy reserves
4. **Round up** - Better to overestimate than underestimate
5. **Learn from history** - Track how long similar tasks actually took
6. **Break down big tasks** - If estimating 3+ hours, consider splitting
7. **Energy varies** - Same task might need different energy at different times
8. **Context matters** - Interruption-prone environments need more buffer time
