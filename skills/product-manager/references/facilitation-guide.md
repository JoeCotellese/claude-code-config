
# Prioritization Facilitation Guide
This guide helps product managers run effective prioritization sessions with stakeholders, whether using RICE, Kano, Value/Effort, MoSCoW, or Weighted Scoring frameworks.

---

## Before the Session

### 1. Define the Scope
- What are we prioritizing? (Features, bugs, tech debt, experiments)
- What time horizon? (Next sprint, quarter, year)
- What resources are available? (Team size, budget)

### 2. Gather Input
- Collect candidate items from all sources (user feedback, stakeholders, engineering, support)
- Pre-estimate effort with engineering (rough T-shirt sizes)
- Gather relevant data (usage analytics, support tickets, revenue impact)

### 3. Choose the Framework
| Situation | Recommended Framework |
|-----------|----------------------|
| Quick team alignment | Value/Effort Matrix |
| Quantitative comparison | RICE Scoring |
| Understanding user perception | Kano Model |
| Fixed deadline scoping | MoSCoW |
| Multiple custom criteria | Weighted Scoring |

### 4. Prepare Materials
- List of items to prioritize (15-30 max per session)
- Framework template (spreadsheet, Miro, whiteboard)
- Data/context for each item
- Timer for timeboxing

### 5. Invite the Right People
- Decision maker (tie-breaker authority)
- Engineering lead (effort estimates)
- Design lead (user experience perspective)
- Business stakeholder (revenue/strategy perspective)
- Customer-facing rep (user feedback perspective)

Keep groups small (5-7 people). Larger groups slow decisions.

---

## Running the Session

### Opening (10 min)

**Set context:**
- "We're here to prioritize X for the next Y"
- "We have Z items to evaluate"
- "We'll use [framework] because..."

**Establish ground rules:**
- One conversation at a time
- Disagree on ideas, not people
- Data over opinions when available
- Timebox discussions (2-3 min per item)
- Parking lot for tangents

**Calibrate the framework:**
- Walk through criteria definitions
- Score 2-3 reference items together to anchor expectations

### Scoring/Sorting (30-60 min)

**For individual scoring (RICE, Weighted):**
1. Silent scoring (5-10 min) - Each person scores independently
2. Reveal scores - Show all scores simultaneously
3. Discuss outliers - Focus on items with high variance (>2 points difference)
4. Converge - Agree on final scores through discussion

**For group sorting (Value/Effort, MoSCoW):**
1. Quick placement - Go item by item, place with minimal discussion
2. Review disagreements - Return to items where placement differed
3. Adjust and finalize

### Discussion Facilitation

**When someone dominates:**
- "Let's hear from others who haven't spoken"
- "I want to get [quiet person]'s perspective on this"

**When discussion goes in circles:**
- "We've heard both sides. Let's vote and move on."
- "Let's timebox this to 2 more minutes, then decide."

**When stuck on one item:**
- "Let's flag this for follow-up research and continue."
- "What information would resolve this disagreement?"

**When emotions run high:**
- Acknowledge: "I can see this is important to you."
- Refocus: "Let's return to the data. What do we know about user impact?"
- Defer: "Let's take a 5-minute break and revisit with fresh eyes."

### Closing (10 min)

**Summarize results:**
- Read back the top priorities
- Note any items flagged for research
- Confirm next steps

**Reality check:**
- "Does this feel right?"
- "Are there any must-haves we've accidentally deprioritized?"
- "Can we realistically deliver these in [timeframe]?"

**Document decisions:**
- Capture final scores/placements
- Record key reasoning (especially for contentious items)
- Note who was in the room

## Probing Questions

Use these to challenge assumptions and deepen understanding:

### For Value/Impact

**User impact:**
- "How many users are affected by this problem?"
- "How often do they encounter it?"
- "What's their current workaround?"
- "Would they pay for this? Switch products for it?"

**Business impact:**
- "How does this affect revenue?"
- "What's the cost of not doing this?"
- "Does this open a new market or segment?"
- "Is this blocking a strategic partnership?"

**Competitive:**
- "Do competitors have this?"
- "Is this table stakes or differentiation?"
- "What happens if we delay 6 months?"

### For Effort/Complexity

**Scope:**
- "What's the minimum viable version?"
- "Can we split this into smaller deliverables?"
- "What can we cut without losing core value?"

**Technical:**
- "What's the biggest unknown?"
- "Do we have the right expertise?"
- "What dependencies exist?"
- "What's the risk of this breaking something?"

**Resources:**
- "Who needs to be involved?"
- "What's the opportunity cost?"
- "Do we have capacity right now?"

### For Confidence

**Evidence:**
- "What data supports this?"
- "Have we validated this with users?"
- "What assumptions are we making?"
- "What would change if we're wrong?"

**Historical:**
- "Have we tried similar things before?"
- "What happened with [analogous feature]?"

## Handling Disagreements

### When Stakeholders Disagree

**1. Clarify the disagreement:**
- "Let me make sure I understand. You're saying X, and you're saying Y?"
- "Are we disagreeing on facts, priorities, or approach?"

**2. Surface underlying concerns:**
- "What's driving your perspective?"
- "What's the worst case if we go the other direction?"
- "What would need to be true for you to change your position?"

**3. Find common ground:**
- "What do you both agree on?"
- "Is there a way to address both concerns?"

**4. Use data as tiebreaker:**
- "Let's see what the data says about this."
- "Can we run a quick experiment to test this?"

**5. Escalate if needed:**
- "We're at an impasse. [Decision maker], we need your call here."
- Document the disagreement and decision rationale.

### When Engineering and Product Disagree on Effort

**Common causes:**
- Different understanding of scope
- Different assumptions about approach
- Different quality bars

**Resolution:**
- Break down the estimate: "What takes the most time?"
- Clarify scope: "Are we talking about the same feature?"
- Find the tradeoff: "What would make this smaller?"

### When Business Pushes for Unrealistic Scope

**Approach:**
1. Acknowledge the desire: "I understand why that's important."
2. Present reality: "Here's what we can deliver in this timeframe."
3. Offer options: "We can do A by date X, or A+B by date Y."
4. Let them choose: "What's most important for the business?"

## After the Session

### Document Decisions

Create a record that includes:
- Date and attendees
- Framework used
- Final prioritized list with scores/placements
- Key reasoning for top items
- Items flagged for research
- Disagreements and how they were resolved

### Communicate Results

- Share summary with broader team
- Update roadmap/backlog
- Close the loop with stakeholders who weren't in the room

### Follow Up on Parking Lot

- Schedule research for unresolved questions
- Plan follow-up discussions for deferred decisions
- Track outcomes to improve future estimates

---

## Common Facilitation Mistakes

### 1. Not Timeboxing
Discussions expand to fill available time. Use a visible timer.

### 2. Letting HiPPOs Dominate
The Highest Paid Person's Opinion shouldn't auto-win. Require data and reasoning.

### 3. Premature Consensus
Silence isn't agreement. Explicitly ask: "Does anyone see this differently?"

### 4. Changing Criteria Mid-Session
If the framework isn't working, finish the session and revise for next time. Don't switch mid-stream.

### 5. Ignoring Emotion
Priorities are emotional. Acknowledge feelings before redirecting to data.

### 6. No Follow-Through
The session is pointless if decisions aren't implemented. Assign owners and deadlines.
